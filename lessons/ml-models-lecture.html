1. A model is a line; the error is MSE

Fit the line by hand. The residual bars are what the model gets wrong; MSE is their mean square. Then show the least-squares line: the best any line can do.

2. Gradient descent adjusts the weights

Instead of guessing w and b, compute the gradient of the MSE and step against it. Watch the update formula with real numbers, then raise η until it diverges.

3. Logistic regression: learning to separate two classes

Same idea, different loss: the line is now a boundary, the sigmoid turns the score into a probability, and gradient descent on the log-loss rotates the line into place.

4. Naive Bayes: classify by probabilities

No boundary is learned directly: each class gets a mean and variance per feature, and Bayes' rule combines prior and likelihoods into a posterior for any point.

5. Decision tree: split and gain

A tree asks threshold questions. Choose the root split yourself, read Gini or entropy of parent and children and the gain, then let the tree grow deeper.

6. k-means: clustering without labels

Two steps, alternating: assign points to the nearest centroid, move centroids to their means. Inertia only falls; restart to see a different local optimum.
